## Implemented and closed findings

A 65,536-interval diagnostic probe found a planning regression: a warm middle-leading
slice took ~1.29 s and 327,680 page reads, despite decoding only 2,048 rows; serial
selection took ~0.016 s. Full canonical validation already proves raw-key ordering.
Authenticated binary searches now skip impossible leading prefixes/suffixes, while
non-leading axes retain conservative summaries and exact residual evaluation.

An initial correction still let a sequential tail decode an unrelated suffix.
The tail now stops at a verified leading-window end. A tail ending at the source's
last ordinal still validates its pending terminator. The same diagnostic output
now needs ~1.5 ms, ~200 index-page reads and 2,048 decoded rows. These small probe
measurements are diagnostic, not substituted for the committed-source scale gate.

Three new boundary tests cover late seeks without a directory prescan, interior
range-union gaps, empty symbols, proven suffix skips and pending terminators after
a sequential window. Existing source/corruption/fallback/resource and acknowledged-
progress tests remain enabled. New work counters distinguish binary probes and
bulk skips; one current span and one transient probe remain root charged. No new
dependency, postings, parallel scan or file-format change is introduced.

`python tools/dev.py m5-check --profile scale` passes all eight cases with three
independent build/reopen/cold trials per case, plus first and three warm queries
per validated session. Every Rust worker uses one 256 MiB root and self-verifies
both hard/soft 512 MiB Linux RLIMIT_AS limits. wait4 RSS, sampled VM/descriptors/I/O,
12 GiB owned index quota, two file handles and a 1024-byte cache are checked.

Largest raw and dictionary source inputs independently exceed four times the

subset exceeds four times the cap. Largest tracked roots remain below 1.3 MB and
observed worker RSS below 15 MB, with fixed 64 MiB growing-RSS allowances enforced.

Full pinned-native validation checks original UEL IDs, all record keys and numeric
bits. Rust label streaming checks every dictionary label/ID; native eager metadata
verification has its explicitly separate 16 GiB limit (observed RSS ~3.46 GB).
That reference exception is not attributed to the Rust 512 MiB workers. Complete
native/serial/indexed ordered digests match, including all-row scans, narrow leading,
non-leading, misses, skew, head/zero/exact limits and late slices. Sources remain
unchanged; Python independently checks source SHA-256 against sidecar headers.
Artifacts are hashed and retired only after complete validation.

## Costs and break-even

Medians below summarize the largest case in each class: three cold serial/build/
strict-open/first-query trials and nine warm queries. Cold uses a fresh process and
per-file fsync/POSIX_FADV_DONTNEED; eviction is advisory and device caches are
uncontrolled. First indexed queries follow explicit strict replay and may benefit
from its source cache warming. Warm queries reuse the same validated session.

| Class | Cold serial s | Build s | Strict reopen s | Warm s | Reopen / build+reopen break-even queries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Raw 36M | 5.692 | 20.831 | 20.559 | 0.001903 | 4 / 8 |
| Zlib 36M | 5.215 | 7.715 | 7.738 | 0.007033 | 2 / 3 |
| Dictionary universe 36M | 0.326 | 7.963 | 7.942 | 0.000896 | 25 / 49 |
| Dense sidecar 8.4M | 1.477 | 83.695 | 73.466 | 0.002075 | 50 / 107 |

The largest raw leading slice returns exactly 2,048 rows. Serial decodes 36M rows,
reads 4,536,000,173 source bytes and fills 138,428 decoded buffers. Indexed queries
decode 8,323 rows, read 1,114,112 source bytes and ~70 index pages, and fill 34
buffers. The gate requires >=2× median warm latency and >=4× complete source work
reductions, including index-page physical reads; all cases pass. Dictionary-universe
queries synthesize IDs/zero values without encoded record payload: only decoded-row
and latency comparisons apply, separately from full physical dictionary streaming.

Strict reopen hashes the entire source and replays the indexed symbol. Build and
validation costs are substantial, especially with one-record checkpoints. Break-even
includes those costs and the first-query/warm difference; warm speedups alone do
not establish cold performance. Skew/non-leading matrices are measured in three
trials, with cache state and counters retained. No general string/mapped adapter
throughput parity, label-lookup speedup or cross-workload latency claim is made.
